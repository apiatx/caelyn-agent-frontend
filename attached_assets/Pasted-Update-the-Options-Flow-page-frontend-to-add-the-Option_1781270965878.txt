Title: Options Guide

What Is an Option?

* An option is a contract that gives you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an asset at a set price within a specific timeframe.
* Each standard options contract represents 100 shares.

Main Types of Options

* Call Option: The right to buy shares at a set price. Used when you think the stock may go up.
* Put Option: The right to sell shares at a set price. Used when you think the stock may go down.

Core Contract Terms

* Strike Price: The agreed-upon price where the option can buy or sell the underlying stock.
* Premium: The price paid for the option contract.
* Expiration Date: The deadline before the contract expires.
* Open Interest: The number of outstanding contracts that still exist.
* Volume: The number of contracts traded today.

Moneyness

* In-The-Money / ITM: The option has intrinsic value.

  * Calls: strike is below current stock price.
  * Puts: strike is above current stock price.
* Out-Of-The-Money / OTM: The option has no intrinsic value.

  * Calls: strike is above current stock price.
  * Puts: strike is below current stock price.
* At-The-Money / ATM: Strike is very close to current stock price.

The Greeks

* Delta / Δ: How much the option price may move for a $1 move in the stock. A 0.45 delta call moves roughly $0.45 for a $1 stock move.
* Gamma / Γ: How quickly Delta changes as the stock moves.
* Theta / Θ: Daily time decay. This is the bleed. The closer expiration gets, the more dangerous this can become.
* Vega: Sensitivity to implied volatility.
* Rho: Sensitivity to interest rates, usually less important for short-term flow but more relevant for LEAPS.

IV / Implied Volatility

* IV shows how much volatility the market is pricing into the option.
* High IV means premium is expensive.
* Buying high-IV options before a catalyst can be risky because IV can collapse even if the stock moves in the right direction.

Buyer vs Seller

* Buying / Long Options: You pay the premium. Risk is limited to the premium paid. Calls can have large upside.
* Selling / Writing Options: You collect premium but take on obligations. This can carry much higher risk.

Footer disclaimer:
“This guide is educational only and not financial advice. Options are risky and can expire worthless.”
